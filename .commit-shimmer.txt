Stop the board shimmering: irregular ground grain and an even camera scale

"Very dizzying, like the pixels." Two causes, both real, and they compounded.

1. MOIRE from a regular micro-pattern on the floor.

   The floor carried a 4x4 ordered-dither mask at 16% density. That is a regular grid of dots, and
   the board is drawn at a non-integer scale, so the two interfere: a regular micro-pattern sampled
   at a fractional scale produces moire - large, slow-moving interference bands across the whole
   screen. The floor is the largest surface in the game, so this was the largest source of it.

   The grain is now placed by an integer hash at 5% density instead. An irregular texture has no
   spatial frequency to interfere with, so it reads as grain at any scale.

   The test suite now asserts this: `tests/terrain.test.mjs` measures the grain density per residue
   class mod 4 and fails if any class carries more than 3x the mean. A 4x4 ordered dither scores
   zero on thirteen of the sixteen, so the old mask fails that assertion outright. It also bounds
   the density (2-10%) and the contrast (under 0.16), so it cannot be "fixed" by making the texture
   invisible instead.

2. AN ARBITRARY FRACTIONAL ZOOM.

   The wheel multiplied the zoom by 1.08, so it landed on values like 0.78, 0.92, 1.17 and 1.44.
   At those scales world pixels do not land on whole screen pixels: some source pixels cover two
   screen pixels and their neighbours cover one, and the grid the art was drawn on stops existing.
   While the camera moved, that irregular grid crawled.

   `src/engine/PixelScale.ts` now holds the policy, pure and testable without a canvas:

     * `snapZoom` - magnification uses a whole number of screen pixels per world pixel, minification
       uses the reciprocal. Nothing in between: at 1.5x two world pixels land on three screen
       pixels, alternating between one and two each, which is exactly the irregular grid.
     * `snapWorld` - camera coordinates snapped so world blits land on whole pixels.
     * `defaultZoomFor` - the startup zoom is an even scale at every viewport width.

   `render` draws with the snapped zoom while the interpolation still drives the transition, so the
   camera still eases but every frame it draws is on an even scale. Degenerate input (0, negative,
   NaN, Infinity) falls back to 1 rather than passing a broken transform to the canvas.

   `tests/pixel-scale.test.mjs` adds five cases: the scale is always whole, an in-between scale such
   as 1.5 is never reachable, twenty wheel notches in each direction stay on the ladder, the ladder
   ascends and allows both zooming out and in, and degenerate input cannot produce a broken
   transform.

Two smaller high-frequency patterns went with them: the stat bar's `repeating-linear-gradient` of
2px stripes became a flat fill with two inset edges (a dense regular pattern in a small, bright,
always-visible element), and the cliff weathering cracks no longer march on an exact 7px stride.

Also fixed by testing: the diamond's south vertex could be left unpainted by the scanline fill,
which is a one-pixel hole straight through the board.

One thing tried and reverted, worth recording. Sizing the canvases in device pixels so one canvas
pixel is one device pixel is correct on a display scale of 100% or 200% and wrong on 125% or 250%:
the backing store would be a whole multiple of the CSS box while the display is not, so the browser
rescales fractionally anyway and the canvas stops lining up with the viewport. At CSS sizing the
upscale is a fixed pattern rather than a moving one, and a fixed pattern over irregular art is not
something the eye reads as shimmer. On a display set to a fractional scale, setting it to 100% is
the one remaining improvement available outside the game.
