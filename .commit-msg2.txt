Rebuild the board floor as pixel-art tiles instead of canvas vector art

The ground was the last part of the game drawn with vector canvas calls:
createLinearGradient for the top face, beginPath/fill for the cliffs and their strata,
fillRect and arc and ellipse for the biome surface detail, and ctx.ellipse for the shoreline
surf. Every one of those antialiases, so the floor was the only soft-edged art in a game whose
structures, trees and characters are all hard-edged pixel art.

IsometricTerrainPainter replaces all of it with a plain RGBA buffer built from the same
IsoSurface primitives the structures and trees use, so the ground now obeys the same rules as
everything standing on it.

Geometry is unchanged and cannot change: the projection is 2:1 dimetric with a 96x48 tile, so
the top face is a rhombus with a 48 half-width and a 24 half-height, the sprite is 104x82, and
the rhombus is centred at (52, 28). The engine now imports TERRAIN_BLIT_X/Y and
TERRAIN_CLIFF_H instead of repeating 52/28/18, so the painter's geometry and the renderer's
blit offset can no longer drift apart.

Lighting. A horizontal face has no wall normal, so its light direction has to come from
bevels: the two west edges take the lit tone and the two east edges the shaded one. Bevelling
only the upper two edges was the first attempt and it lit the tile from the north vertex, which
reads as top-down light and contradicts every roof and wall standing on it. The body is then
shaded with a 4x4 Bayer ordered dither that thins the lit tone from the west and thickens the
shaded tone toward the east - a gradient would band across 312 repeated tiles, and a flat fill
would read as a colour swatch.

This also fixes a real palette bug. For snow, coral, cavern, castle and celestial the authored
palette gives the RIGHT cliff face the lighter colour, so painting cliffLeft/cliffRight
literally lit those five biomes from the wrong side. The pair is now read as the biome's rock
colour family - the darker member is the body - and both faces are derived from it, which keeps
every biome's hue while guaranteeing the west face is the lit one. This is the same class of
bug as the cottage chimney in the structure set, and the same fix: derive the ramp, do not
trust an ad-hoc pair.

The shoreline surf is now a 2:1 isometric ring drawn as integer 2x1 blocks with a scrolling
dash pattern, replacing two ctx.ellipse calls.

Verification: 105 tests, all green. tests/terrain.test.mjs adds 15 cases over 60 tiles
(15 biomes x flat/cliff x day/night): byte-for-byte PNG comparison, the projection geometry,
that the top face is solid so the sky never shows through a hole in the board, that a flat
tile stops at the south vertex and a cliffed tile spans the full width there and tapers to its
foot, that nothing spills into the sprite padding, that every tile is lit from the left, and
separately that the two cliff faces agree with the top face about the light direction.

npm run gen:terrain exports the tiles; gen:art now runs buildings, foliage and terrain.
