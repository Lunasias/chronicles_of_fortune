Rebuild the board's floor clutter as pixel-art props

The boulders, grass tufts, wildflower patches, shrubs and magic crystals scattered over the
board were drawn with ctx.arc, ctx.ellipse and moveTo/lineTo paths - a dozen path calls per prop
per frame, all of them antialiasing, and none of it cached. A rock sitting in a pixel-art meadow
had soft edges.

IsometricPropPainter produces a fixed 34x34 RGBA buffer per prop, so the clutter is hard-edged
like everything else and IsometricTerrainRenderer blits a cached canvas instead of re-issuing
paths. Five types x 15 biomes x 4 variants x 3 sizes x 2 times of day, all deterministic.

Four decisions worth recording:

* Prop colours are derived from the biome's own floor palette rather than a second hand-written
  table, so a boulder cannot drift out of the biome it is sitting on and a new biome gets
  matching clutter for free.

* Size is a discrete bucket, not a scale factor. The generator picks a continuous 0.85-1.2
  scale; smoothly scaling a pixel-art sprite either blurs it or gives uneven pixel sizes, so the
  scale is quantised onto three baked sizes.

* Props now have night variants. They did not before, which left bright green grass blades
  glowing on a darkened night floor next to night-dimmed tiles.

* The crystal shimmer cycles through four baked frames instead of fading the sprite, so the
  shimmer stays hard-edged too.

Three art bugs surfaced while testing, all of them the same shape as the earlier cottage chimney
and cliff-face bugs - an ad-hoc colour pair trusted instead of a derived ramp:

* The boulder's west facet was darker than its east facet, copied from the vector code. Every
  rock was lit from the right while the tiles under it were lit from the left.

* shift() floors lightness at 6%, so for volcanic obsidian (#09090b) the two darkest steps of a
  rock ramp both clamped to the floor and collapsed onto one colour, leaving a boulder with three
  tones and no crack. toneRamp()/rockLevels() space a ramp by absolute lightness instead, with a
  saturation cap because near-black colours report a misleadingly high HSL saturation and were
  coming out vivid purple. The floor cliffs use the same ramp.

* A one-pixel grass blade has no surface to shade, so shading blade-by-blade left a tuft with no
  readable light direction at all. Vegetation is now shaded positionally across the whole clump,
  and the flower rosette and crystal cluster needed the same treatment - the tallest crystal and
  the biggest blossom were both landing east of centre.

Also fixed: the crystal fell back to one shared blue when a biome had no glow, which made grass,
desert, castle, cavern and the crystal cavern grow identical crystals; the flower's four variants
differed only in hue, so variants 0 and 2 were byte-identical; and the flower's three blossoms
were arranged symmetrically.

Verification: 116 tests, all green. tests/props.test.mjs adds 11 cases covering all 1800
combinations for size, shading, left lighting, cast shadow, seating on the anchor, type
distinguishability by 4x4 coverage grid, per-variant distinctness, per-biome and day/night
variation, the crystal pulse, and the unknown-biome fallback.

Props are the one art set with no committed PNGs: nothing ever loads them from disk, so a
byte-for-byte test would protect nothing, and 600-odd prop files would be a lot of binary noise.
npm run gen:props writes review sheets instead.
